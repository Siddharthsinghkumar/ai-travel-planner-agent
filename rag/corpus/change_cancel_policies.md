# Change and Cancellation Policies — Knowledge Base

## US DOT 24-Hour Rule

1. The US Department of Transportation (DOT) requires all airlines selling tickets to or from the United States to offer a 24-hour free cancellation policy, provided the ticket was purchased at least 7 days before the flight's departure date. This applies to all fare classes, including basic economy.

2. Airlines may comply with the 24-hour rule in one of two ways: either by allowing free cancellation with a full refund within 24 hours of booking, or by allowing passengers to hold a reservation at the quoted fare for 24 hours without payment. Most major US carriers offer the full refund option.

3. The 24-hour rule applies to tickets purchased directly from the airline. Tickets purchased through third-party OTAs (Online Travel Agencies) may be subject to the OTA's own cancellation policies in addition to the airline's policy. The DOT rule still applies, but the refund process may take longer when booked through an intermediary.

4. The 24-hour clock starts from the time of purchase, not midnight of the booking date. A ticket purchased at 3:00 PM on Monday can be cancelled without penalty until 3:00 PM on Tuesday. Airlines typically process refunds within 7 business days for credit card purchases.

## Change and Cancellation by Carrier

5. American Airlines: Basic Economy tickets are non-changeable and non-refundable. Main Cabin and above tickets can be changed with no change fee for domestic and short-haul international flights, but the passenger must pay any fare difference. International long-haul tickets may still incur change fees of USD 150-400 depending on the route and fare class.

6. Delta Air Lines: Basic Economy tickets are non-changeable and non-refundable for domestic flights. For international Basic Economy tickets, changes are permitted with a fee starting at USD 99. Main Cabin and above have no change fees for domestic flights, but fare differences apply. Delta credits from cancelled tickets are valid for 12 months from the original ticket issue date.

7. United Airlines: Basic Economy tickets are non-changeable and non-refundable. Standard Economy and above can be changed with no change fee for domestic flights, but fare differences apply. United travel credits are valid for 12 months from the date of original ticket issuance. United offers a flexible booking policy for some international routes with reduced change fees.

8. Lufthansa: Economy Light fares are non-refundable and non-changeable. Economy Classic and Flex fares can be changed for a fee plus any fare difference. Cancellation refunds depend on fare conditions — Flex fares are fully refundable, Classic fares are partially refundable, and Light fares are non-refundable. Change fees range from EUR 100-300 depending on route and fare class.

9. Emirates: Special fares are non-refundable and non-changeable. Saver fares can be changed for a fee of USD 100-300 plus fare difference. Flex and Flex Plus fares can be changed for free or with a reduced fee. Cancellation refunds vary by fare type — Flex Plus fares are fully refundable, Flex fares are partially refundable, and Saver fares are non-refundable.

## OTA Change and Cancellation Policies

10. Expedia: Cancellation policies depend on the individual airline or hotel's rules as displayed at booking. Expedia charges no additional cancellation fee beyond what the provider charges. Refunds are processed within 10-14 business days. Expedia's "Book Now, Pay Later" option allows some reservations to be cancelled without charge up to a specified deadline.

11. Booking.com: Cancellation policies vary by property and are clearly displayed at booking. Many properties offer free cancellation up to 24-48 hours before check-in. Non-refundable rates are clearly marked and cannot be cancelled. Booking.com does not charge additional cancellation fees beyond the property's policy.

12. Kayak: As a metasearch engine, Kayak redirects users to the booking provider (airline, OTA, or hotel) for actual reservations. Cancellation policies are determined by the provider where the booking is completed. Kayak does not process cancellations or refunds directly.

## Refund Eligibility and Travel Credit

13. Refundable tickets are typically 2-5 times more expensive than non-refundable tickets but allow full cancellation with a refund to the original payment method. Refundable tickets are common in Business and First Class and are available as an option in Economy on most carriers.

14. Travel credit (also called flight credit or travel voucher) is issued when a non-refundable ticket is cancelled. The credit is typically valid for 12 months from the date of original ticket issuance and can only be used by the original passenger. Some airlines have extended credit validity during the pandemic period.

15. Cash refund vs. travel credit: If the airline cancels the flight or makes a significant schedule change (typically 2+ hours for domestic, 4+ hours for international), passengers are entitled to a full cash refund regardless of fare type. If the passenger initiates the cancellation, non-refundable tickets receive travel credit while refundable tickets receive cash refunds.

16. Partial refunds: If a passenger cancels only one segment of a multi-city or round-trip itinerary, the remaining segments may be cancelled automatically by the airline. Passengers should check with the airline before cancelling any segment. Some airlines allow partial cancellations with a proportional refund or credit.

## Irregular Operations (IROPS) Rebooking Rights

17. When a flight is cancelled or significantly delayed due to circumstances within the airline's control (mechanical issues, crew scheduling), the airline is obligated to rebook the passenger on the next available flight at no additional cost. If the airline cannot rebook within a reasonable time, passengers may request a full refund.

18. When a flight is cancelled or delayed due to circumstances outside the airline's control (weather, air traffic control, security), the airline is still obligated to rebook the passenger but is not required to provide compensation beyond the rebooking. Some airlines voluntarily offer meal vouchers or hotel accommodation even for weather-related disruptions.

19. Involuntary rerouting: If the airline reroutes a passenger through a different city or on a different carrier due to a cancellation, the passenger is entitled to the same cabin class and comparable travel time. If the rerouted flight arrives significantly later than the original, the passenger may be entitled to compensation depending on the jurisdiction.

20. Connection protection: If a passenger misses a connecting flight due to a delay on the first leg of their journey (on the same ticket), the airline is responsible for rebooking the passenger on the next available connection at no cost. This applies even if the missed connection is on a partner airline within the same alliance.

21. Refund rights for significant delays: Under US DOT regulations, if an airline cancels a flight or makes a significant change to the schedule, passengers are entitled to a full refund to their original form of payment, even for non-refundable tickets. The DOT has not defined a specific threshold for "significant change," but delays of 3+ hours are generally considered significant.

22. Rebooking on partner airlines: When an airline cannot accommodate a passenger on its own flights within a reasonable time, it may rebook the passenger on a partner airline or even a competing carrier. This is at the discretion of the airline and is more common during major disruptions. Passengers can request partner airline rebooking but the airline is not obligated to honor the request.

23. Baggage during IROPS: If a passenger is rebooked on an alternate flight due to a cancellation, checked baggage will typically be rerouted automatically. However, passengers should verify baggage routing with the airline agent, especially when rebooked on a different carrier. Baggage may need to be collected and re-checked in some cases.

24. Hotel and meal vouchers during IROPS: US airlines are not legally required to provide hotel or meal vouchers for delays within their control, but most major carriers have policies to provide them. American, Delta, and United typically provide hotel vouchers for overnight delays within their control and meal vouchers for delays exceeding 3 hours. European carriers are required to provide care under EU 261/2004.

25. Travel insurance and IROPS: Trip insurance policies typically cover trip interruption and delay, providing reimbursement for additional expenses incurred during irregular operations. However, insurance does not replace the airline's obligations — passengers should first seek rebooking and care from the airline, then file an insurance claim for uncovered expenses.
