# Travel Safety and Security Rules — Knowledge Base

1. Travel advisories: The US State Department issues travel advisories for every country on a scale of 1-4: Level 1 (Exercise Normal Precautions), Level 2 (Exercise Increased Caution), Level 3 (Reconsider Travel), and Level 4 (Do Not Travel). Check the State Department website before booking international travel. Travel insurance may not cover trips to Level 3 or 4 countries.

2. Passport security: Keep your passport in a hotel safe when not needed. Carry a photocopy or digital photo of your passport's identification page separately from the original. Some travelers use a money belt or hidden pouch for their passport when exploring cities. RFID-blocking wallets can protect against electronic skimming of passport and credit card data.

3. Pickpocket prevention: Common pickpocket locations include crowded tourist attractions, public transportation, and restaurants. Techniques include distraction (asking for directions, spilling something), bumping, and cutting bags. Use cross-body bags with zippers, keep wallets in front pockets, and be aware of your surroundings in crowded areas.

4. Hotel safety: Use the deadbolt and chain lock when in your room. Do not open the door for unannounced visitors — call the front desk to verify. Use the room safe for valuables. Check for hidden cameras in rental properties (Airbnb) by looking for unusual objects near beds, bathrooms, and living areas. Report suspicious devices to the platform and local authorities.

5. ATM safety: Use ATMs inside banks or in well-lit, busy areas. Avoid standalone ATMs in tourist areas which may have skimming devices. Cover the keypad when entering your PIN. Notify your bank of travel plans before departure to avoid your card being blocked for suspicious foreign transactions.

6. Scam awareness: Common travel scams include the "friendship bracelet" scam (someone ties a bracelet on your wrist then demands payment), the "found ring" scam (someone picks up a ring and offers to sell it to you), and the "petition" scam (someone asks you to sign a petition while an accomplice pickpockets you). Be polite but firm in declining unsolicited interactions.

7. Emergency numbers: 911 is the emergency number in the US and Canada. 112 is the emergency number in the EU and works from any phone (even without a SIM card). 999 is used in the UK. Other countries have different emergency numbers — research before travel. Save the local emergency number and your country's embassy/consulate number in your phone.

8. Travel insurance emergency assistance: Most travel insurance policies include 24/7 emergency assistance hotlines. These can help with medical referrals, emergency cash transfers, legal assistance, and evacuation coordination. Save the emergency assistance number in your phone and carry a printed copy of your policy.

9. Digital security while traveling: Use a VPN on public WiFi to encrypt your internet traffic. Avoid accessing banking or sensitive accounts on public WiFi without a VPN. Enable two-factor authentication on all accounts. Consider using a dedicated travel laptop or phone with minimal data. Back up important files to cloud storage before departure.

10. Solo travel safety: Share your itinerary with a trusted contact at home. Check in regularly. Avoid walking alone at night in unfamiliar areas. Trust your instincts — if a situation feels unsafe, leave. Solo female travelers should research destination-specific safety concerns and consider joining group tours for certain activities. Register with your country's traveler enrollment program (STEP for US citizens).

11. Natural disaster preparedness: Research the natural disaster risks for your destination (hurricanes, earthquakes, tsunamis, volcanic eruptions, wildfires). Know the evacuation routes and emergency shelters. Sign up for local emergency alerts. Keep a small emergency kit in your bag (water, snacks, first aid, flashlight, cash). Follow local authorities' instructions during an emergency.

12. Political unrest and protests: Avoid areas of political unrest and protests, even if they appear peaceful. Protests can escalate quickly. Monitor local news and your country's travel advisories. If caught in a protest, leave the area immediately and do not photograph or engage with protesters. Contact your embassy if you need assistance.

13. Kidnapping and express kidnapping: Express kidnapping (forcing victims to withdraw money from ATMs) occurs in some countries (Mexico, Colombia, South Africa). Use ATMs inside banks during business hours. Avoid displaying expensive jewelry, watches, or electronics. Use registered taxis or ride-hailing apps rather than hailing cabs on the street.

14. Cybersecurity at border crossings: Border agents in some countries (US, UK, Australia) may search electronic devices. Password-protect your devices and consider carrying a "clean" travel device with minimal data. Encrypted devices may be subject to additional scrutiny. Some countries (China, Russia) have been known to install malware on devices during border searches.

15. Travel document backup: Before departure, email yourself copies of your passport, visa, travel insurance policy, itinerary, and emergency contacts. Store copies in a secure cloud service (Google Drive, Dropbox, iCloud). Carry a printed copy of your passport's identification page and travel insurance policy separately from the originals. This ensures you can access your documents even if your belongings are stolen.
