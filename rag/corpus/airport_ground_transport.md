# Airport and Ground Transport Rules — Knowledge Base

1. Major international airports typically require passengers to arrive at least 2 hours before domestic flights and 3 hours before international flights. Security screening times can vary significantly — during peak hours (6-9 AM and 4-7 PM), allow an additional 30-45 minutes.

2. TSA PreCheck members can use expedited security lanes at participating US airports, typically reducing screening time to under 5 minutes. The application costs USD 78 and is valid for 5 years. Global Entry members automatically receive TSA PreCheck benefits.

3. CLEAR is a biometric identity verification service available at select US airports that allows members to bypass the ID check line. CLEAR costs USD 189 per year and can be combined with TSA PreCheck for the fastest screening experience.

4. Airport lounge access can be purchased at the door (typically USD 50-75 per visit) or through annual memberships (Priority Pass at USD 99-469/year, airline-specific lounges at USD 500-650/year). Some premium credit cards include complimentary lounge access.

5. Many airports offer left luggage or baggage storage services for passengers with long layovers. Costs typically range from USD 5-15 per bag per day. Some airports also offer shower facilities for transit passengers at USD 20-40 per use.

6. Ground transportation from airports includes taxis, rideshare (Uber/Lyft), airport shuttles, and public transit. Taxi fares from major airports to city centers typically range from USD 25-80 depending on distance and city. Rideshare pricing is dynamic and can surge during peak hours.

7. Airport hotels near major hubs offer day rates (4-8 hour blocks) for transit passengers, typically costing USD 50-120. Some hotels offer free shuttle service from the airport terminals. Booking in advance is recommended during peak travel seasons.

8. Many airports have dedicated transit hotels inside the terminal (e.g., YOTEL at JFK, Minute Suites at DFW) that can be booked by the hour. These are ideal for layovers of 4-8 hours and typically cost USD 40-80 per hour.

9. Airport WiFi is increasingly free at major international airports, but speeds and reliability vary. Some airports offer premium paid WiFi with higher bandwidth for USD 5-15 per day. Business travelers should consider a mobile hotspot as a backup.

10. Duty-free shopping at international airports is available to departing passengers only. Purchases must be carried in sealed tamper-evident bags (STEBs) if connecting through another airport. Liquids purchased duty-free may be subject to security screening at connecting airports.
