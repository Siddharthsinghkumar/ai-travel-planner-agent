# In-Flight Experience and Service Rules — Knowledge Base

1. Seat pitch (legroom) varies significantly by airline and cabin class. Economy seat pitch ranges from 28-34 inches, with budget carriers (Spirit, Ryanair) at the low end and full-service carriers (Delta, Emirates) at the high end. Premium Economy typically offers 36-40 inches, Business Class 55-80 inches, and First Class 60-86 inches.

2. In-flight entertainment (IFE) systems vary from seatback screens on wide-body aircraft to streaming to personal devices on narrow-body aircraft. Some budget carriers charge for IFE access (USD 5-10). Download entertainment to your device before boarding as a backup.

3. Meal service on international flights typically includes at least one meal and a snack on flights over 6 hours. Meal quality and variety vary by airline and cabin class. Special meals (vegetarian, kosher, halal, gluten-free, diabetic) must be requested at least 24-48 hours before departure.

4. Wi-Fi on board is increasingly available but varies in quality and cost. Some airlines offer free messaging-only Wi-Fi (JetBlue, Delta on select aircraft), while others charge USD 5-30 for full internet access. Satellite-based Wi-Fi (GoGo, Viasat, Panasonic) is faster than ground-based systems but can still be slow compared to ground connections.

5. Cabin pressure and humidity: Aircraft cabins are pressurized to an altitude of 6,000-8,000 feet, which can cause mild hypoxia symptoms (fatigue, headache) in sensitive individuals. Cabin humidity is typically 10-20%, which can cause dry skin and eyes. Stay hydrated and consider using saline nasal spray on long flights.

6. Jet lag occurs when crossing 3+ time zones and is more severe when traveling east. Symptoms include fatigue, insomnia, and difficulty concentrating. Mitigation strategies include adjusting your sleep schedule before travel, staying hydrated, avoiding alcohol and caffeine during the flight, and getting sunlight exposure at your destination.

7. Ear pressure during takeoff and landing can cause discomfort or pain. Chewing gum, swallowing, or performing the Valsalva maneuver (pinching nose and gently blowing) can help equalize pressure. Infants may experience more discomfort — feeding during takeoff and landing can help.

8. Overhead bin space is limited and operates on a first-come, first-served basis. Boarding earlier in the boarding group increases the likelihood of finding overhead bin space near your seat. Some airlines (Spirit, Frontier) charge for overhead bin access and only allow one free personal item that fits under the seat.

9. Flight attendants are trained in first aid, CPR, and emergency procedures. Most wide-body aircraft carry an AED (automated external defibrillator) and a medical kit. If you have a medical condition, inform the flight attendants at boarding so they can be prepared to assist if needed.

10. Unruly passenger incidents are taken seriously and can result in fines, criminal charges, and being placed on a no-fly list. The FAA can fine passengers up to USD 37,000 per incident for interfering with flight crew. Airlines have zero-tolerance policies for physical aggression and threats.

11. Service animals are permitted in the cabin on all US airlines under DOT regulations. Airlines may require advance notice (48 hours) and documentation of the animal's training and health. Emotional support animals are no longer recognized as service animals by most US airlines and must travel as pets.

12. Involuntary bumping occurs when a flight is oversold and the airline cannot find enough volunteers to give up their seats. Compensation is regulated by the DOT (see disruption rights). To reduce the risk of being bumped, check in early, arrive at the gate on time, and avoid being the last passenger to board.
