# Regional Travel Guides — Americas Knowledge Base

1. US domestic travel: The US has 50 states with diverse climates and travel experiences. Domestic flights are the primary mode of long-distance travel, with major carriers including American, Delta, United, Southwest, and Jetstar. Southwest includes two free checked bags and has no change fees. TSA PreCheck costs USD 78 for 5 years and is available at most major airports.

2. Canada entry requirements: US citizens can enter Canada visa-free for up to 6 months. An Electronic Travel Authorization (eTA) is required for air travel (CAD 7, valid 5 years) but not for land or sea entry. Canada has strict customs regulations — declare all food, plants, and animal products. Cannabis is legal in Canada but cannot be transported across the border.

3. Mexico entry requirements: US citizens can enter Mexico visa-free for up to 180 days. A Forma Migratoria Multiple (FMM) tourist card is required and is typically included in airline ticket prices. Land border crossings require separate purchase of the FMM (approximately USD 30). Mexico has no exit tax for tourists.

4. Central America travel: Guatemala, Honduras, El Salvador, Nicaragua, Costa Rica, and Panama each have different visa requirements. US citizens can enter Costa Rica visa-free for 90 days, Panama for 180 days, and Guatemala/Honduras/El Salvador/Nicaragua for 90 days under the CA-4 agreement. The CA-4 agreement treats the four countries as a single zone for immigration purposes.

5. South America visa requirements: US citizens can enter Brazil visa-free (until April 2025, when visas will be reinstated), Argentina for 90 days, Chile for 90 days, Colombia for 90 days, Peru for 183 days, and Ecuador for 90 days. Bolivia requires a visa-on-arrival for US citizens costing USD 160. Reciprocity fees may apply for some nationalities.

6. Caribbean travel: Most Caribbean islands allow US citizens to enter visa-free for 30-90 days. Some islands require a passport valid for 6 months beyond the date of entry. US territories (Puerto Rico, US Virgin Islands) do not require a passport for US citizens. Cruise passengers may have different entry requirements than air travelers.

7. Alaska cruise season runs from May to September. Most cruises depart from Seattle, Vancouver, or Seward. A passport is required for cruises that stop in Canada (most Alaska cruises). The Inside Passage offers scenic glacier viewing and wildlife (whales, bears, eagles). Book cruises 6-12 months in advance for the best prices and cabin selection.

8. Hawaii travel: Domestic flights from the US mainland take 5-8 hours. Inter-island flights (Hawaiian Airlines, Southwest) cost USD 50-150 and take 30-60 minutes. Hawaii has no sales tax but has a high cost of living. Rental cars are recommended for exploring the islands — book in advance as supply can be limited. The best time to visit is April-May and September-October for lower prices and fewer crowds.

9. US national parks: The America the Beautiful Pass (USD 80/year) provides entry to all national parks and federal recreational lands. Individual park entrance fees range from USD 15-35 per vehicle. Popular parks (Yellowstone, Yosemite, Grand Canyon, Zion) require advance reservations for camping and lodging during peak season. Some parks (Arches, Glacier, Rocky Mountain) require timed entry reservations.

10. Road trips in the US: The most iconic road trips include Route 66 (Chicago to Los Angeles, 2,448 miles), Pacific Coast Highway (California coast, 655 miles), Blue Ridge Parkway (Virginia to North Carolina, 469 miles), and Going-to-the-Sun Road (Montana, 50 miles). Gas prices vary significantly by state — plan fuel stops in cheaper states when possible.

11. US hotel taxes: Hotel occupancy taxes vary by city and state, typically ranging from 6-18% of the room rate. These taxes are usually not included in the advertised price and are added at checkout. Some cities have additional tourism fees or convention center taxes. Resort fees (USD 15-50 per night) are common in Las Vegas, Hawaii, and major resort destinations.

12. Argentina travel: The official exchange rate and the "blue dollar" (informal market rate) can differ significantly. Western Union offices offer rates close to the blue dollar. Credit card payments now receive a favorable exchange rate (MEP rate). Buenos Aires is known for its steak, tango, and European-style architecture. Iguazu Falls (on the border with Brazil) is one of the world's most impressive waterfalls.

13. Peru travel: Machu Picchu requires advance tickets and has a daily visitor limit of 4,500. The Inca Trail requires a licensed guide and permits sell out 6-8 months in advance. Altitude sickness is common in Cusco (3,400m) — spend 1-2 days acclimatizing before hiking. Coca tea is a traditional remedy for altitude symptoms. Lima is known as the gastronomic capital of South America.

14. Chile travel: Chile stretches 4,300 km from north to south, encompassing the Atacama Desert (world's driest), the Andes mountains, Patagonia, and Easter Island. Domestic flights are the most efficient way to travel between regions. Santiago is the main international gateway. The best time to visit Patagonia is November-March (summer in the Southern Hemisphere).

15. Colombia travel: Colombia has transformed from a dangerous destination to a popular tourist spot. Major destinations include Cartagena (colonial walled city), Medellin (innovation hub), Bogota (capital with museums), and the Coffee Region (eje cafetero). US citizens can enter visa-free for 90 days. Safety has improved significantly but petty crime remains a concern in major cities.
