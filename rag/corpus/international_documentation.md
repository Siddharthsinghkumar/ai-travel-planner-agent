# International Travel Documentation Rules — Knowledge Base

1. Passport validity requirements: Most countries require your passport to be valid for at least 6 months beyond your planned departure date. Some countries require only 3 months (Schengen Area, UK), while others require 6 months (China, Thailand, Vietnam, Indonesia). Always check the specific requirement for your destination.

2. Passport pages: Many countries require your passport to have at least 2-4 blank pages for entry and exit stamps. Some countries (South Africa, Kenya) require a full blank page (not a half-page). If your passport is running low on pages, apply for a renewal before travel.

3. Visa application processing times vary widely: e-visas typically take 3-5 business days, embassy visas can take 2-8 weeks, and visa-on-arrival is immediate but requires queuing at the airport. Apply for visas well in advance of your travel date to account for delays.

4. Some countries require proof of onward travel (a return or onward ticket) as a condition of entry. This is common in Southeast Asia (Thailand, Indonesia, Philippines), Central America, and some Caribbean nations. Airlines may deny boarding if you cannot show proof of onward travel.

5. Proof of sufficient funds: Some countries require travelers to show proof of sufficient funds for their stay. This can be in the form of cash, credit cards, or bank statements. Thailand requires 10,000 THB per person or 20,000 THB per family. Australia may ask for proof of AUD 1,000-5,000 depending on length of stay.

6. Travel authorization programs: Many countries have implemented electronic travel authorization systems for visa-exempt visitors. Examples include ESTA (US, USD 21), eTA (Canada, CAD 7), ETA (Australia, AUD 20), NZeTA (New Zealand, NZD 17-23), and ETIAS (Schengen Area, EUR 7, starting 2025). These are not visas but pre-screening authorizations.

7. Dual nationality travelers should enter and exit a country using the passport of that country if they hold citizenship. Using a foreign passport to enter your country of citizenship can cause complications. Some countries (China, India) do not recognize dual nationality and may consider you solely a citizen.

8. Children traveling: Many countries require additional documentation for children traveling with one parent, without parents, or with non-parent guardians. This can include notarized consent letters from the non-traveling parent(s), birth certificates, and custody documents. Check the specific requirements for your destination.

9. Passport photo requirements vary by country: US passport photos must be 2x2 inches with a white background, taken within the last 6 months. UK passport photos must be 45x35mm with a light grey or cream background. Many countries now require digital photos for e-visa applications with specific resolution and file size requirements.

10. Emergency passport replacement: If your passport is lost or stolen while abroad, contact your country's nearest embassy or consulate immediately. Emergency passports are typically valid for a single journey home and may take 1-3 days to issue. File a police report for stolen passports as it may be required for the replacement application.
