# Regional Travel Guides — Middle East and Africa Knowledge Base

1. UAE (Dubai/Abu Dhabi) entry: US citizens receive a free visa-on-arrival valid for 30 days. The UAE has strict customs regulations — medications containing codeine, tramadol, and some ADHD medications are controlled substances and require a prescription and prior approval. Alcohol is legal in licensed venues but requires a license to purchase for home consumption (Dubai abolished this requirement in 2023).

2. Saudi Arabia tourist visa: Available as an e-visa for USD 80 (includes mandatory health insurance), valid for 1 year with multiple entries of up to 90 days per visit. Saudi Arabia has opened to tourism since 2019. Key destinations include Riyadh, Jeddah, AlUla (ancient Nabataean city), and the Red Sea coast. Dress codes are more relaxed than in the past but modest clothing is still expected in public.

3. Egypt entry: Visa-on-arrival costs USD 25 and is valid for 30 days. An e-visa is also available. The Egyptian Museum in Cairo houses the world's largest collection of Pharaonic antiquities. The new Grand Egyptian Museum (GEM) near the Pyramids of Giza is partially open. Nile cruises between Luxor and Aswan are a popular way to visit ancient temples.

4. Morocco entry: US citizens can enter visa-free for up to 90 days. Morocco uses the Moroccan Dirham (MAD), which is a closed currency (cannot be exchanged outside Morocco). The medinas (old cities) of Fez, Marrakech, and Chefchaouen are UNESCO World Heritage Sites. Haggling is expected in souks — start at 30-40% of the asking price.

5. South Africa entry: US citizens can enter visa-free for up to 90 days. South Africa has strict passport requirements — passports must have at least 2 blank pages and be valid for 30 days beyond the intended departure date. Kruger National Park is one of Africa's largest game reserves. Cape Town offers Table Mountain, Robben Island, and the Cape Winelands.

6. Kenya entry: US citizens must obtain an Electronic Travel Authorization (eTA) before travel, costing USD 30. The eTA replaced the e-visa system in 2024. Kenya is known for its safari destinations (Maasai Mara, Amboseli, Tsavo) and the Great Migration (July-October). Mombasa offers beach holidays on the Indian Ocean coast.

7. Tanzania entry: Visa-on-arrival costs USD 50 (single entry) or USD 100 (multiple entry, valid 12 months). An e-visa is also available. Tanzania is home to Serengeti National Park, Ngorongoro Cradle, Mount Kilimanjaro (Africa's highest peak), and Zanzibar. Zanzibar follows the same visa requirements as mainland Tanzania.

8. Israel entry: US citizens can enter visa-free for up to 90 days. Israel requires all visitors to have a passport valid for 6 months beyond the date of entry. Some Arab countries may deny entry to travelers with Israeli entry stamps — Israel now issues entry cards instead of stamping passports to avoid this issue. Security screening at Ben Gurion Airport is thorough and can take 1-2 hours.

9. Jordan entry: Visa-on-arrival costs JOD 40 (approximately USD 56) for a single-entry visa valid for 30 days. The Jordan Pass (JOD 70-80) includes the visa fee and entry to 40+ attractions including Petra, Wadi Rum, and Jerash. Petra is one of the New Seven Wonders of the World and requires at least one full day to explore.

10. Qatar entry: US citizens can enter visa-free for up to 30 days. Hamad International Airport in Doha is consistently ranked among the world's best. Qatar Airways offers a free stopover package for passengers with layovers of 12+ hours, including hotel accommodation and city tours. The 2022 FIFA World Cup infrastructure has left Qatar with world-class facilities.

11. Oman entry: Visa-on-arrival or e-visa costs OMR 20 (approximately USD 52) for a 30-day single-entry visa. Oman is known for its dramatic landscapes (wadis, deserts, mountains), traditional souks, and hospitality. Muscat is the capital and offers the Sultan Qaboos Grand Mosque and Mutrah Souq. A 4x4 vehicle is recommended for exploring the interior.

12. Nigeria entry: US citizens must obtain a visa before travel through the Nigerian embassy or consulate. Tourist visas cost approximately USD 160 and processing takes 3-5 business days. Nigeria is Africa's most populous country and largest economy. Lagos is the commercial capital and offers vibrant nightlife, music, and food. Abuja is the purpose-built capital.

13. Ghana entry: Visa is required for US citizens and can be obtained at the Ghanaian embassy or as a visa-on-arrival (pre-approval required). Ghana is known for its historical sites related to the transatlantic slave trade (Cape Coast Castle, Elmina Castle), vibrant culture, and the "Year of Return" initiative encouraging diaspora visits.

14. Ethiopia entry: E-visa is available online for USD 52 (30-day single entry) or USD 72 (90-day single entry). Ethiopia is home to the rock-hewn churches of Lalibela, the Simien Mountains, and the Danakil Depression (one of the hottest places on Earth). Addis Ababa is the diplomatic capital of Africa and headquarters of the African Union.

15. Rwanda entry: Visa-on-arrival costs USD 50 for most nationalities. Rwanda is known for gorilla trekking in Volcanoes National Park (permit costs USD 1,500 per person), the genocide memorial in Kigali, and its clean streets (plastic bags have been banned since 2008). Rwanda has positioned itself as a conference and eco-tourism destination.
