# Visa Requirements — Knowledge Base

## US Passport Visa-Free Destinations

1. United States passport holders can travel to the United Kingdom for up to 6 months without a visa for tourism, business meetings, or short-term study. An Electronic Travel Authorization (ETA) will be required starting in 2025 for visa-free visitors, costing GBP 10 and valid for multiple entries over two years.

2. Canada allows US citizens to enter visa-free for stays up to 6 months. An Electronic Travel Authorization (eTA) is required for air travel, costing CAD 7 and valid for 5 years or until passport expiry. eTA is not required for land or sea entry. Processing is typically instant but can take up to 72 hours.

3. Australia requires US citizens to obtain an Electronic Travel Authority (ETA) subclass 601 before travel, which costs AUD 20 and is valid for 12 months with stays of up to 3 months per visit. The ETA is electronically linked to the passport and processing is usually completed within minutes.

4. Mexico allows US citizens to enter visa-free for tourism or business for up to 180 days. A Forma Migratoria Multiple (FMM) tourist card must be completed, which is typically included in the airline ticket price for air travel. Land border crossings require separate purchase of the FMM.

5. Japan permits visa-free entry for US citizens for stays up to 90 days for tourism, business, or visiting friends and relatives. No visa application is required, but travelers must have a valid passport and proof of onward travel. Working or studying requires a separate visa.

6. South Korea allows visa-free entry for US citizens for up to 90 days for tourism or business. Starting in 2025, the K-ETA (Korea Electronic Travel Authorization) will be required, costing KRW 10,000 and valid for 2 years. US citizens are currently exempt from K-ETA requirements through 2025.

7. New Zealand requires US citizens to obtain an NZeTA (New Zealand Electronic Travel Authority) before travel, costing NZD 17 via the app or NZD 23 via web form. It is valid for 2 years and allows multiple visits of up to 3 months each. An International Visitor Conservation and Tourism Levy (IVL) of NZD 35 is also required.

## Schengen Area Rules

8. The Schengen Area comprises 29 European countries with a common visa policy. US citizens can enter visa-free for up to 90 days within any 180-day period for tourism, business, or family visits. The 180-day clock is a rolling window, not a calendar period.

9. The 90/180 rule applies across the entire Schengen Area as a single zone. Time spent in France counts toward the same 90-day limit as time spent in Germany, Italy, or any other Schengen country. Travelers must track their days carefully to avoid overstaying.

10. Overstaying the Schengen 90-day limit can result in fines, deportation, and a ban on re-entry for up to 5 years. Border authorities may stamp passports on entry and exit to track compliance. Some countries have automated border gates that record entry electronically.

11. Starting in 2025, visa-exempt travelers to the Schengen Area must obtain ETIAS (European Travel Information and Authorization System) authorization before travel. ETIAS costs EUR 7, is valid for 3 years or until passport expiry, and allows multiple short stays. Processing is typically within minutes.

12. Schengen countries include: Austria, Belgium, Bulgaria, Croatia, Czech Republic, Cyprus, Denmark, Estonia, Finland, France, Germany, Greece, Hungary, Iceland, Italy, Latvia, Liechtenstein, Lithuania, Luxembourg, Malta, Netherlands, Norway, Poland, Portugal, Romania, Slovakia, Slovenia, Spain, Sweden, and Switzerland.

13. Non-Schengen EU countries (Ireland, Romania, Bulgaria, Cyprus) have separate visa policies. Time spent in these countries does not count toward the Schengen 90-day limit. Romania and Bulgaria joined Schengen for air and sea travel in March 2024 but maintain separate land border controls.

## Visa-on-Arrival Destinations

14. Egypt offers visa-on-arrival for US citizens at major airports, costing USD 25 and valid for a single entry of up to 30 days. An e-visa is also available online for USD 25 and is recommended to avoid airport queues. Sinai-only travel (Sharm El-Sheikh) may qualify for a free 15-day entry permit.

15. Indonesia provides visa-on-arrival for US citizens at designated entry points, costing IDR 500,000 (approximately USD 32) and valid for 30 days with one extension possible for another 30 days. The visa can be obtained at major airports including Ngurah Rai (Bali) and Soekarno-Hatta (Jakarta).

16. Turkey offers an e-visa for US citizens valid for 90 days within a 180-day period, costing USD 50. The e-visa must be obtained before travel at the official e-visa website. Visa-on-arrival is no longer available for US citizens; the e-visa is mandatory.

17. Kenya requires US citizens to obtain an Electronic Travel Authorization (eTA) before travel, costing USD 30 and valid for 90 days. The eTA replaced the e-visa system in 2024. Processing typically takes 2-3 business days. Visa-on-arrival is no longer routinely available.

18. Tanzania offers visa-on-arrival for US citizens at major entry points, costing USD 100 for a multiple-entry tourist visa valid for 12 months. A single-entry visa costs USD 50. An e-visa is also available and recommended for faster processing. Zanzibar follows the same visa requirements as mainland Tanzania.

## Embassy Visa Required Destinations

19. China requires US citizens to obtain a visa before travel through a Chinese embassy or consulate or authorized visa service center. Tourist (L) visas typically cost USD 140 and are valid for 10 years with multiple entries of up to 60 days per visit. Processing takes 4-5 business days.

20. Russia requires US citizens to obtain a visa before travel, which requires an invitation letter (tourist voucher) from a registered Russian tour operator or hotel. Tourist visas cost approximately USD 160 and processing takes 4-20 business days depending on service level. As of 2024, visa processing may be subject to additional scrutiny.

21. India requires US citizens to obtain a visa before travel. An e-Tourist Visa is available online for USD 25-80 depending on duration (30 days, 1 year, or 5 years) and is the most common option. Processing takes 3-5 business days. Traditional visa applications through consulates are also available for longer stays.

22. Vietnam offers an e-visa for US citizens valid for up to 90 days with single or multiple entry, costing USD 25 for single entry or USD 50 for multiple entry. Processing takes 3-5 business days. Visa-on-arrival with a pre-approved letter is also available but requires flying into designated airports.

23. Brazil reinstated visa requirements for US citizens starting April 10, 2025. US citizens will need to obtain a visa before travel through a Brazilian consulate or the upcoming e-visa system. The tourist visa is typically valid for 10 years with multiple entries of up to 90 days per visit.

## Transit Visa Scenarios

24. A transit visa may be required when passing through a country en route to a final destination, even if the traveler does not leave the airport. The United States requires all travelers (including those in transit) to have a valid visa or ESTA, regardless of whether they leave the airport.

25. The United Kingdom requires a Direct Airside Transit Visa (DATV) for nationals of certain countries transiting through UK airports, even if they do not pass through border control. US citizens are exempt from this requirement. However, if the traveler needs to pass through UK border control to change terminals, a Visitor in Transit visa may be needed.

26. China offers a 24-hour, 72-hour, or 144-hour visa-free transit policy for citizens of many countries, including the US, at designated airports. Travelers must have a confirmed onward ticket to a third country and remain within the designated city or region. The 144-hour transit is available at major hubs including Beijing, Shanghai, and Guangzhou.

27. Australia requires a transit visa (subclass 771) for travelers transiting through Australian airports, even for layovers under 8 hours, unless they are eligible for the Transit Without Visa (TWOV) facility. US citizens are eligible for TWOV if transiting within 8 hours with a confirmed onward ticket and not leaving the transit area.

28. Schengen Area transit: US citizens do not need a transit visa for layovers within the Schengen Area if they remain in the international transit area of the airport. However, if the traveler must pass through immigration to collect baggage or change airports (e.g., CDG to Orly in Paris), they are entering the Schengen Area and the 90-day clock starts.

29. Dubai (UAE) offers free visa-on-arrival for US citizens valid for 30 days, which covers transit passengers who wish to leave the airport. For layovers under 24 hours where the traveler stays airside, no visa is required. Emirates and other airlines may offer complimentary transit hotel arrangements for long layovers.

30. Japan allows visa-free transit for US citizens. If the traveler remains in the international transit area, no entry formalities are required. If the traveler wishes to leave the airport during a layover, they enter under the standard 90-day visa-free entry rules.
