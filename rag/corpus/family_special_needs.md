# Family and Special Needs Travel Rules — Knowledge Base

1. Infants under 2 years can travel on a parent's lap for free on domestic flights and for 10% of the adult fare on international flights. However, the FAA recommends using an approved child safety seat for infants and toddlers. Car seats must be FAA-approved (look for the label) and fit in the aircraft seat (typically up to 16 inches wide).

2. Unaccompanied minor services are offered by most airlines for children aged 5-14 traveling alone. Fees range from USD 50-150 each way. The service includes dedicated staff escort, priority boarding, and in-flight supervision. Children under 5 cannot travel unaccompanied. Some airlines (Southwest, JetBlue) do not allow unaccompanied minors on connecting flights.

3. Family boarding: Some airlines offer family boarding between priority boarding and general boarding. Southwest allows families with children 6 and under to board after A-group and before B-group. Delta and American allow families with young children to board together but do not have a designated family boarding group.

4. Stroller policies: Most airlines allow one stroller per child to be checked for free in addition to the standard baggage allowance. Gate-checking is available — you can use the stroller through the airport and leave it at the gate for loading into the cargo hold. Compact umbrella strollers can often be stored in the overhead bin.

5. Breastfeeding and pumping on flights: TSA allows breast milk, formula, and baby food in quantities greater than 3.4 oz in carry-on bags. These items are not subject to the 3-1-1 liquids rule but must be declared for separate screening. Most airlines allow breastfeeding on board. Some airports have nursing rooms in terminals.

6. Traveling with children who have autism or sensory processing disorders: TSA Cares provides assistance for travelers with disabilities and medical conditions. Call 72 hours before travel at 1-855-787-2227. Some airports offer hidden disability lanyards (Sunflower Lanyard program) to discreetly indicate that the traveler may need additional assistance or patience.

7. Wheelchair assistance: All US airlines are required to provide wheelchair assistance from check-in to the aircraft door and from the aircraft to baggage claim. Requests should be made at least 48 hours before departure. Airlines must provide aisle chairs for boarding and deplaning passengers who cannot walk. Wheelchairs are transported free of charge.

8. Service animals: Under DOT regulations, airlines must allow service dogs to travel in the cabin at no charge. Airlines may require advance notice (48 hours) and documentation (DOT Service Animal Air Transportation Form). Emotional support animals are no longer recognized as service animals by US airlines and must travel as pets.

9. Oxygen and medical equipment: Portable oxygen concentrators (POCs) are allowed on most airlines if they are FAA-approved. Airlines do not provide oxygen for passenger use — passengers must bring their own POC. CPAP machines are allowed on board and can be used during flight. Batteries for medical devices must be carried in the cabin.

10. Traveling with elderly family members: Request wheelchair assistance even if the traveler can walk short distances — airports can involve long walks between gates. Book aisle seats for easier access to the lavatory. Consider booking direct flights to reduce the stress of connections. Travel insurance with medical coverage is essential for elderly travelers.

11. Pregnancy travel: Most airlines allow pregnant women to fly up to 36 weeks for single pregnancies and 32 weeks for multiple pregnancies. A medical clearance certificate may be required after 28 weeks. The best time to travel during pregnancy is the second trimester (14-28 weeks) when morning sickness has usually subsided and the risk of complications is lowest.

12. Food allergies on flights: Airlines are not required to provide allergen-free meals, but many will accommodate nut allergies by not serving nut products on the flight (though they cannot guarantee a nut-free environment). Passengers with severe allergies should bring their own food and carry epinephrine auto-injectors. Notify the airline at least 48 hours before departure.

13. Traveling with pets in the cabin: Most airlines allow small pets (under 8 kg including carrier) in the cabin for a fee of USD 95-125 each way. The carrier must fit under the seat in front of you. Only 2-4 pets are allowed per flight in the cabin, so book early. Some breeds (brachycephalic dogs and cats) are restricted from cargo travel due to breathing difficulties.

14. Special meals on flights: Most airlines offer special meals that must be requested at least 24-48 hours before departure. Options include: vegetarian (VGML), vegan (VJML), kosher (KSML), halal (MOML), gluten-free (GFML), diabetic (DBML), low-sodium (LSML), child (CHML), and infant (BBML). Special meals are typically served before the standard meal service.

15. Traveling with cremated remains: Cremated remains are allowed in carry-on or checked baggage. The container must be scannable (wood or plastic — not metal). TSA may need to open the container for inspection. Carry a copy of the death certificate and cremation certificate. Check with the airline for specific requirements.
