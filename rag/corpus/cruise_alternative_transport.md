# Cruise and Alternative Transport Rules — Knowledge Base

1. Cruise booking windows: The best prices for Caribbean cruises are typically found 60-90 days before sailing. For Mediterranean and Alaska cruises, book 6-12 months in advance. Last-minute deals (within 30 days) can offer 30-50% discounts but have limited cabin selection. Repositioning cruises (when ships relocate between seasons) offer the best per-day value.

2. Cruise cabin categories: Interior (no window, cheapest), Oceanview (window or porthole), Balcony (private veranda), Suite (larger space, priority boarding, concierge service). The price difference between interior and balcony can be USD 500-2,000 per person for a 7-night cruise. Balcony cabins are recommended for scenic itineraries (Alaska, Mediterranean, Norwegian fjords).

3. Cruise gratuities: Most cruise lines charge automatic daily gratuities of USD 14-20 per person per day, distributed to cabin stewards, dining staff, and behind-the-scenes crew. These can usually be prepaid or adjusted at the guest services desk. Specialty dining and spa services often include an additional 18-20% gratuity.

4. Cruise beverage packages: Unlimited beverage packages cost USD 50-100 per person per day and include alcoholic and non-alcoholic drinks. Calculate whether the package is worth it based on your drinking habits (typically 5+ alcoholic drinks per day to break even). Some cruise lines offer drink packages as part of booking promotions.

5. Cruise shore excursions: Book through the cruise line for guaranteed return to the ship if the excursion runs late. Independent excursions are typically 20-40% cheaper but carry the risk of missing the ship's departure. Popular shore excursions include snorkeling, historical tours, zip-lining, and food tours. Book popular excursions 3-6 months in advance.

6. Cruise dress codes: Most cruise lines have formal nights (1-2 per 7-night cruise) where passengers dress up. Formal attire ranges from cocktail dresses and suits to tuxedos and gowns. Some cruise lines (Norwegian, Virgin Voyages) have no formal dress code. Casual nights typically require resort casual (no shorts or flip-flops in the main dining room).

7. Amtrak: The US national passenger rail system serves 46 states and over 500 destinations. The Acela Express (Boston-Washington DC) reaches speeds of 150 mph. The California Zephyr (Chicago-Emeryville) is considered one of the most scenic train routes in the world. Amtrak offers a USA Rail Pass (USD 499-1,299 for 15-45 days of unlimited travel).

8. Greyhound and FlixBus: Intercity bus travel is the most affordable long-distance transportation option in the US. FlixBus offers fares as low as USD 5-20 for short routes and USD 30-80 for cross-country trips. Greyhound serves over 3,800 destinations in the US. Both offer free WiFi and power outlets on most routes.

9. Ferry services: The Alaska Marine Highway System connects coastal communities in Alaska and serves as a scenic alternative to flying. Washington State Ferries is the largest ferry system in the US. The Staten Island Ferry (NYC) is free and offers views of the Statue of Liberty. International ferries connect the UK to France, Spain, and the Netherlands.

10. Private jet charter: Services like NetJets, Wheels Up, and JSX (semi-private) offer alternatives to commercial airlines. JSX operates "hop-on" flights from private terminals with no TSA lines, free checked bags, and complimentary drinks. Prices start at USD 149-249 each way for routes in Texas, California, Colorado, and the Northeast.

11. Helicopter transfers: Helicopter services connect major airports to city centers in some locations. BLADE offers helicopter transfers from JFK/LGA/EWR to Manhattan (USD 195-395 per person). HeliFlite operates in the Northeast. These services can save 1-2 hours compared to ground transportation during peak traffic.

12. Car-sharing services: Turo (peer-to-peer car rental) and Zipcar (short-term car rental) offer alternatives to traditional car rental. Turo prices are typically 10-30% lower than traditional rental companies. Zipcar offers hourly rentals (USD 10-15/hour) ideal for short trips within a city. Both require a valid driver's license and clean driving record.

13. Ride-hailing internationally: Uber operates in 70+ countries. Grab is the dominant ride-hailing app in Southeast Asia (Singapore, Malaysia, Thailand, Vietnam, Indonesia, Philippines). DiDi operates in Australia, Brazil, and parts of Latin America. Bolt is popular in Europe and Africa. Download the local ride-hailing app before arriving.

14. Bicycle touring: Long-distance bicycle touring is a growing travel trend. Popular routes include the EuroVelo network (17 routes across Europe), the Great Divide Mountain Bike Route (US, 2,700 miles), and the Shimanami Kaido (Japan, 70 km across islands). Bicycle touring requires physical preparation, route planning, and appropriate gear. Average daily distance is 40-80 km.

15. Walking and hiking pilgrimages: The Camino de Santiago (Spain, 500-800 km depending on starting point) is the most famous walking pilgrimage. The Kumano Kodo (Japan, 70 km) and the Via Francigena (Italy, 1,900 km from Canterbury to Rome) are other notable routes. These require physical preparation, proper footwear, and advance planning for accommodation. Many routes offer credencial (pilgrim passport) for stamp collection and certificate upon completion.
