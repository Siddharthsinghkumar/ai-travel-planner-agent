# Regional Travel Guides — Europe Knowledge Base

1. Eurail Pass: The Eurail Global Pass allows unlimited train travel across 33 European countries for a set number of days within a validity period. Prices start at EUR 243 for 4 days within 1 month (youth under 28) and EUR 323 for adults. Seat reservations are required on many high-speed trains (TGV, ICE, Eurostar) and cost EUR 10-40 extra.

2. Schengen 90/180 rule: Non-EU citizens can stay in the Schengen Area for up to 90 days within any 180-day period. The 180-day period is a rolling window — each day of stay counts toward the 90-day limit. Overstaying can result in fines, deportation, and a ban on re-entry. Use the EU's online Schengen calculator to track your days.

3. Eurostar connects London to Paris (2h 16m), Brussels (1h 53m), and Amsterdam (3h 55m). Passengers must clear passport control before boarding. Tickets should be booked 90-120 days in advance for the best prices. Standard Premier includes a meal and drinks. Children under 4 travel free without a seat.

4. Budget airlines in Europe: Ryanair, easyJet, Wizz Air, and Vueling offer fares as low as EUR 10-30 for short-haul flights. However, these carriers charge for checked baggage (EUR 20-50), seat selection (EUR 5-20), and priority boarding (EUR 5-15). Always compare the total cost including fees when comparing with full-service carriers.

5. European city taxes: Many European cities charge a tourist tax per person per night, typically EUR 1-7. This is usually collected by the hotel at check-in or check-out and is not included in the booking price. Cities with notable tourist taxes include Rome (EUR 3-7), Paris (EUR 0.80-4.50), Amsterdam (EUR 3-7%), and Venice (EUR 1-5 plus the new day-tripper fee).

6. VAT refund for non-EU residents: Purchases over EUR 100-175 (varies by country) can be eligible for a VAT refund of 10-20% when leaving the EU. Request a tax-free form at the store, get it stamped at customs on departure, and submit it for a refund. Processing can take 4-8 weeks and refunds are typically 70-85% of the VAT amount (the rest is the refund company's fee).

7. European train travel tips: Book high-speed train tickets (TGV, AVE, Frecciarossa) 2-3 months in advance for the best prices. Regional trains do not require advance booking and have fixed prices. Night trains (ÖBB Nightjet, SNCF Intercités de Nuit) offer couchettes (shared bunks) and sleeper cabins, saving on accommodation costs.

8. UK entry requirements: US citizens can enter the UK visa-free for up to 6 months. Starting in 2025, an Electronic Travel Authorization (ETA) will be required, costing GBP 10 and valid for 2 years. The UK is not part of the Schengen Area, so time spent in the UK does not count toward the Schengen 90-day limit.

9. European health insurance: The European Health Insurance Card (EHIC) provides access to state healthcare for EU citizens traveling within the EU. US citizens should purchase travel insurance with medical coverage of at least EUR 30,000 (required for Schengen visa applicants). US Medicare provides no coverage outside the United States.

10. Driving in Europe: An International Driving Permit (IDP) is required in many European countries (Italy, Spain, Austria, Greece) in addition to your home country license. Car rental prices range from EUR 20-80 per day depending on the country and season. Manual transmission is standard — automatic cars cost 20-50% more. Toll roads are common in France, Italy, and Spain.

11. European museum passes: Many cities offer museum passes that provide free or discounted entry to major attractions. Paris Museum Pass (EUR 62 for 2 days, EUR 77 for 4 days, EUR 92 for 6 days) covers 50+ museums. London Pass (GBP 80-140 per day) covers 80+ attractions. Berlin WelcomeCard includes public transport and museum discounts.

12. Tipping in Europe: Tipping customs vary by country. In France and Italy, service is included in the bill (service compris) and tipping is optional (round up or leave EUR 1-2). In Germany, rounding up to the nearest euro or adding 5-10% is common. In the UK, 10-15% is standard in restaurants. In Scandinavia, tipping is not expected as service charges are included.

13. European plug adapters: Most of Europe uses Type C (two round pins) or Type F (Schuko, two round pins with earth clips) plugs at 230V/50Hz. The UK uses Type G (three rectangular pins) at 230V/50Hz. Switzerland uses Type J (three round pins in a triangle). Always check the plug type for your specific destination.

14. Best time to visit Europe: Peak season (June-August) offers the best weather but highest prices and crowds. Shoulder season (April-May, September-October) offers good weather, fewer crowds, and lower prices. Low season (November-March) offers the lowest prices but some attractions may have reduced hours or be closed. Christmas markets (late November-December) are a popular winter attraction in Germany, Austria, and France.

15. European budget travel: Hostels cost EUR 15-40 per night for a dorm bed. Budget hotels cost EUR 50-100 per night. Mid-range hotels cost EUR 80-150 per night. Daily food budgets: EUR 20-40 for budget (supermarket meals, street food), EUR 40-80 for mid-range (restaurant meals), EUR 80+ for fine dining. Public transportation day passes cost EUR 5-15 in most cities.
